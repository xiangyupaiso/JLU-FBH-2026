      "content": "<title>3.3 Results</title>\n\n# Overview\n\nCirCure was developed through a linked design–build–test–learn cycle. We first defined a complementary antigen panel for triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) and reduced 1,838 possible 9-mer peptides to 15 structurally testable epitopes. These candidates were then converted into a 13-construct single-epitope circRNA library for experimental screening. Round 1 wet-lab validation identified the strongest epitopes from each antigen and guided the construction of the eight-epitope combined vaccine.\n\nThe combined vaccine was subsequently assessed across the complete immune-response chain, from RNA quality and intracellular translation to dendritic-cell maturation, T-cell activation, effector-memory differentiation, cytokine secretion and tumor-cell killing. Additional experiments examined circRNA stability, off-target cytotoxicity and the dose window of the LNP carrier. Finally, a TROP2-positive tumor model was established to evaluate the platform in vivo. Together, these results address whether a multi-antigen circular RNA vaccine can be designed, constructed, functionally validated and evaluated within a traceable experimental framework.\n\nThe results are therefore presented as a continuous evidence chain. Each stage was used to answer a specific question and to determine the design of the next stage: which antigens should be included, which epitopes should be retained, whether the RNA product was correctly formed, whether the combined vaccine activated the intended immune functions, whether tumor-cell killing was selective, and whether the platform could produce an anti-tumor response in vivo.\n\n# Introduction\n\nTNBC is characterized by substantial heterogeneity in tumor-antigen expression, as exemplified by mesothelin expression (Tchou et al., 2012). This creates two related challenges for vaccine design: a single antigen may not be present on every tumor cell, and an epitope that appears promising computationally may not generate a measurable immune response experimentally. We therefore did not begin by selecting one apparently dominant target. Instead, we first asked whether a complementary antigen panel could cover distinct tumor-cell populations and whether short epitopes from these antigens could be reduced to a tractable set for wet-lab testing.\n\nTo address this question, we combined evidence from tissue-level expression, cell-type-resolved expression, patient-level coverage and intact-tissue localization. We then applied allele-specific peptide prediction and structural filtering to the retained antigens. Four candidate proteins were identified through a systematic scoring framework evaluating subcellular localization, tumor specificity, prognostic association, and TNBC therapeutic validation depth (§3.1.1), then tested against four independent lines of evidence before being retained. Every overlapping 9-mer of the four canonical sequences was screened for HLA-A\\*02:01 presentation, and the survivors were checked for loading geometry against the peptide-binding groove. Two filters were applied in series: a sequence-based prediction of HLA-A\\*02:01 presentation, and a structural check that each surviving peptide can physically be loaded into the binding groove. The first reduces the candidate space; the second removes sequences that score well numerically but cannot be presented. \n\n### 3.2.1  IEDB prediction: 1,838 peptides reduced to 17 \n\n**Aim:** Enumerate every possible 9-mer epitope from the four antigens and reduce them to a set small enough to test, keeping those predicted both to bind HLA-A\\*02:01 and to be immunogenic once bound. \n\n**Design & Readout:** All overlapping 9-mers of the four UniProt canonical sequences were screened through the IEDB T Cell Epitope Prediction Class I suite against HLA-A\\*02:01, using NetMHCpan 4.1 EL for binding and elution and NetMHCpan 4.1 BA inside the processing model for predicted IC₅₀. Two numerical gates were applied in order — predicted IC₅₀ < 500 nM, then IEDB immunogenicity score > 0 — followed by a review of each survivor against its position in the source protein (Model: IEDB inputs, parameters, and filtering rules). \n\n**Result :**\n\nTable 6. Within the chosen allele, peptide length was fixed at 9 amino acids, which is the canonical preference but excludes the 8-, 10- and 11-mers that HLA-A\\*02:01 also presents. The topology review is a binary call made from annotated protein features, not a measurement of processing — a peptide classified as transmembrane-derived is excluded on reasoning about its availability, not on evidence that it fails to be presented. The 34 peptides removed at this stage were never tested, so the rule has not itself been checked experimentally. \n\n### 3.2.2  Structural validation: loading geometry of the 17 candidates\n\n**Aim:** Answer one question for each of the 17 survivors: can this peptide actually be loaded into the HLA-A\\*02:01 groove in the correct geometry? A predicted affinity is a number about a sequence; it does not show that the two anchor residues reach their pockets or that the conserved contacts holding the peptide in place are formed. \n\n**Design & Readout:** Each of the 17 peptides was co-folded with HLA-A\\*02:01 in 5 independent models. Three measures were taken on every model and a verdict required to be identical across all five: side-chain burial at positions 2 and 9 (the B and F anchor pockets) at ≥ 90%; at least 10 of the 11 conserved hydrogen bonds present; and a position-9 side-chain fill ratio ≥ 60%, i.e. ≥ 84 Å², which is 60% of the 140 Å² reference buried side-chain area for a leucine anchor. Interface steric clashes were checked and TCR-facing surface area recorded (Model: Structural validation of the 17 candidates). \n\nThe fill-ratio criterion is the one that does work the burial percentage cannot. Burial is saturated — almost every peptide reads 99–100% at position 9, because the residue is enclosed whatever its size. Absolute buried volume is not: a glycine at position 9 contributes 0 Å², an alanine 60 Å², a valine \\~114 Å², a leucine or isoleucine \\~140 Å². A near-100% burial of a small residue means a groove pocket that is enclosed but not filled, which the percentage alone conceals. \n\n**Result:** \n\nFifteen of the 17 candidates passed. The two rejections were both at position 9: FLAEASVRG ends in glycine, which has no side chain and leaves the F pocket empty (0 Å², fill ratio 0%), and HILHVSFLA ends in alanine, filling 43% of the reference volume against the 60% threshold. Both would have been passed by the burial criterion alone — FLAEASVRG's burial at position 9 is undefined for lack of a side chain, and HILHVSFLA reads 99.4%. \n\n这两个图是现在现成的组件\n\n![这张图片为Per-position side-chain burial across the 17 candidates相关的可视化结果，对应文档中提及的Figure 11。图表展示了17个候选表位在5个独立模型上平均的各位置侧链埋藏情况，y轴列出了17个候选肽段，每个肽段按来源抗原分为TROP2、B7-H4、Nectin-4、LIV-1四类，x轴对应表位的P1至P9共9个位置。图中浅绿色带斜线的区域代表甘氨酸，这类氨基酸无侧链；颜色深浅反映侧链埋藏在结合处的百分比，颜色越接近浅白代表埋藏占比越低，颜色越深代表埋藏占比越高，其中位置9是筛选中重点提及的关键位点，图中也呈现了该位置各肽段的侧链埋藏情况。](https://feishu.cn/file/QW0eb0Vfpo6B6bxoT12cbfaInLb)\n\nFigure 11. Round 1 observations and resulting Round 2 design changes.\n\n| Observation | Our reading | Resulting design change |\n|-|-|-|\n| SFC spanning **47–271** across the 13 constructs | In silico prediction **cannot substitute** for immunogenicity validation | Only wet-lab-validated strong epitopes carried forward |\n| **B7-H4 contributed only two** constructs, against three to five for each other antigen | If all four antigens are to be **equally represented** in the cassette, the per-antigen ceiling is **two** | Take each antigen's **two highest-SFC epitopes** — eight in all |\n| K562 killing spanned only **1.41-fold** across the 13 constructs and did not correlate with ELISpot (ρ = 0.43) | A **MHC-I-negative, antigen-negative** target cannot resolve epitopes; a killing assay needs a target carrying both the antigen and the matching HLA type | Round 2 switched to **MDA-MB-231** (HLA-A\\*02:01⁺, TROP2⁺) |\n\n**Why insist on equal representation of all four antigens rather than simply taking the eight highest-SFC epitopes overall.** Triple-negative breast cancer is antigenically heterogeneous: target expression varies between cells within one tumor, and sustained pressure on a single antigen invites antigen-loss escape (Eroles et al., 2026). Covering four antigens in parallel hedges against that. It does **not** broaden population coverage — **every epitope in this project is HLA-A\\*02:01-restricted, so population coverage is bounded by that restriction and is independent of how many antigens are included** (see §3.2.1, Limitations and scope).\n\nThe cost of the rule can be stated exactly, and it turns out to be **zero**. Selecting the eight highest-SFC epitopes without regard to antigen gives **exactly the same eight** (271, 217, 143, 118, 97, 82, 78, 74 — total 1080 SFC): on this dataset the equal-representation rule and the pure ranking rule coincide. The rule would only have bitten one place further down — the ninth epitope by SFC is LIV1-CTL4 at **73**, one point below Nectin4-CTL1 at **74** — so the two criteria were separated here by a single SFC count. We state this rather than claiming the rule did work it did not have to do.\n\nTwo epitopes per antigen rather than one builds in redundancy at the **epitope** level: processing and presentation of any single epitope vary between individuals and can be evaded by a point mutation, so a second epitope from the same antigen provides a fallback. **We did not test this** — no one-epitope-per-antigen control cassette was ever built.\n\nOn this basis we designed the **combined multi-epitope vaccine plasmid**, **pCirCure-circMEV (5267 bp)**, and ordered the complete construct from a commercial gene-synthesis supplier. The two highest-SFC constructs from each antigen were retained: **TROP2-CTL1/CTL2, B7H4-CTL1/CTL2, Nectin4-CTL1/CTL2 and LIV1-CTL2/CTL3**. **Nectin4-CTL1** **is the merged fragment** FQARLRLRVLVPPL, spanning the overlapping predicted epitopes FQARLRLRV and RLRVLVPPL (§3.2.2), so the cassette contains **nine predicted epitopes in eight fragments**. Flexible linkers separate the fragments, and the reading frame was checked across every junction in the design. Five preparations were made for Round 2: **empty vector, TROP2-CTL1, TROP2-CTL2, TROP2-CTL1+2 and combined**. The corresponding commercially supplied plasmids were linearised and purified for T7 in vitro transcription. After DNA-template removal and RNA purification, the precursor RNAs were circularised and treated with RNase R. The release data are in §5.5.\n\n**Two elements of this architecture come from the literature rather than from our own data.** The **flexible linkers** and the **proteasomal-cleavage-guided ordering** are standard practice in multi-epitope vaccine design (Nielsen et al., 2005; Zhou et al., 2025): linkers are intended to prevent junctional neo-epitopes and to preserve cleavage at each epitope boundary. We adopted both, and confirmed computationally that the chosen linkers introduce no novel predicted epitopes — but **we never built a linker-free or differently-ordered version of this cassette, so we have no experimental measurement of what either element contributes.** What our own data do establish is the *selection rule* — which epitopes, and how many per antigen — and that rule is traceable to Table 10. Where a design element rests on the literature rather than on our bench, this page says so.\n\nThe final plasmid is composed as follows. This section reports the six datasets in that order.\n\n**Why TROP2-CTL1 and TROP2-CTL2 are the comparators.** They are the **two strongest single epitopes in the entire Round 1 panel** — 271 and 217 SFC against a panel median of 78 (Table 10). Choosing them sets the comparison at its hardest: comparing against a mid-range epitope would leave the margin open to the choice of comparator. Consequently, **the entire increment from Blank to combined can only come from the epitope-encoding sequence**. Those measurements answer whether the construct works; they do not answer **what dose to use in vivo** or which steps in the delivery-expression-immune chain might **limit efficacy**. We built a mechanistic model to connect cell-level responses to animal dosing and to identify doses worth testing before an animal study.\n\nThe model links **seven processes**: LNP distribution across injection site, lymph nodes, and blood; circRNA expression and innate signaling; antigen presentation; T-cell priming and effector activity; tumor growth; and immunosuppression. Cell viability, killing, DC maturation, and T-cell activation data from Rounds 1 and 2 **constrain the response functions**; published literature on RNA expression kinetics and tumor growth provides the remaining parameters. The model outputs a dose-response landscape and intermediate states at each step, allowing us to trace differences in final tumor size back to delivery, expression, or immune-activation bottlenecks.\n\n## **7.1 Model architecture and calibration strategy**\n\nThe model comprises seven modules with defined interfaces. Module 1 provides dose-response curves from cell assays; Module 2 calculates payload distribution across compartments; Module 3 tracks RNA expression and innate signals; Module 4 connects antigen presentation to effector activity and suppression; Module 5 simulates tumor burden; Module 6 compares doses under uncertainty; Module 7 identifies which parameters and structural choices drive the results.\n\nExperimental measurements constrain viability and killing responses **directly**. DC maturation (CD80/CD86 MFI) and T-cell activation (CD69+ percentage, IFN-γ and GzmB secretion) anchor immune-activation parameters. The **geometric mean** of three markers — percentage of activated cells at 24 h, IFN-γ concentration, and GzmB concentration — defines a **composite effector score**, weighted equally because each captures a distinct facet of the adaptive response and none had prior reason to dominate. Cross-scale parameters linking nanogram-level expression to organ-level effects remain **unvalidated** and are treated as conditional assumptions rather than calibrated quantities.\n\nThe baseline scenario injects **equal doses** subcutaneously on days 0, 7, 14, 21, and 28, with follow-up to day 35. The assay was designed to isolate the carrier: circRNA is itself sensed by cytosolic receptors such as RIG-I/MDA5 (§6.1), so a loaded particle delivers a stimulus an empty one does not, and the ceiling of the whole formulation is a separate quantity. The ceiling of the loaded formulation remains to be defined in a future in vivo dose-escalation study. We therefore describe 200 ng as **the carrier's** safe upper bound and do not extend it to the vaccine.\n\n**Why the in vitro and in vivo dose units are not interconvertible.** The dose unit here is **ng of empty LNP per 10⁴ DCs**; the in vivo dose is **10 µg of loaded circRNA-LNP per animal**. The denominators are entirely different (a defined number of cells versus the distribution volume and total cell count of a whole animal), so the two **cannot be converted into or compared with one another**. This experiment defines an upper bound for the in vitro working dose; the safety of the in vivo dose must be answered by an independent animal toxicology study. We state this explicitly because juxtaposing nanogram-scale in vitro data with microgram-scale in vivo doses is a common but serious interpretive error.\n\n**Other boundaries of the readout.** This is a single cell type at a single time point (24 h). CCK-8 measures mitochondrial dehydrogenase activity, so it reports metabolic viability rather than cell survival and does not distinguish apoptosis from necrosis. Repeat-dose toxicity, immunotoxicity and systemic toxicology are outside this assay and belong to the animal toxicology study above.\n\n# In Vivo: Efficacy and Immune Mechanism\n\nThe in vitro experiments defined several properties of the vaccine material and delivery carrier, but they could not determine whether the platform could affect tumor growth in an intact organism. We therefore built a **TROP2-overexpressing 4T1 line**, established subcutaneous tumors in BALB/c mice, and treated them with **five weekly doses of circRNA-LNP** against PBS and empty-vector controls. Tumor volume was followed by caliper over six weeks; at endpoint we assessed tumor volume, intratumoural CD8 / Granzyme B immunofluorescence, and splenic immunophenotype by flow cytometry with intracellular cytokine staining. The study followed **ARRIVE 2.0** (Percie du Sert et al., 2020), with the design drafted in the NC3Rs Experimental Design Assistant and welfare limits set per UKCCCR/NCRI guidance and body-condition scoring. It was approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of the First Hospital of Jilin University (approval **JDYY20261369**, 27 July 2026; animal use license **SYXK (Ji) 2023-0011**) and follows the **3Rs** and humane endpoints. This section reports the growth curves, the endpoint data and the two mechanism readouts.\n\n**Limitations and scope:**\n\n**The species–HLA mismatch, and how it frames every result in this section.**\n\nCirCure's epitopes were predicted and screened as **human HLA-A\\*02:01-restricted**, but the efficacy study uses **immunocompetent wild-type BALB/c mice (H-2d haplotype)**. Human HLA-A\\*02:01-restricted epitopes cannot, in principle, be efficiently presented by the mouse's own MHC-I molecules.\n\nAll in vivo results in this section should therefore be understood as a **proof of concept for the multi-epitope circRNA platform**. They show that the platform can induce a substantial anti-tumor immune response and restrain tumor growth in a living animal, but that response may include non-specific innate immune effects from the circRNA backbone and adjuvant. What this design reaches is platform-level efficacy. Attributing the effect to antigen-specific, HLA-A*02:01-restricted CTLs takes an HLA-A*02:01 transgenic or humanized mouse together with tetramer staining of antigen-specific CD8⁺ T cells, which is our next step. The remedy is to move the experiment into a mouse system expressing human HLA-A\\*02:01 (an HLA-A\\*02:01 transgenic or humanized mouse), combined with tetramer staining to quantify antigen-specific CD8⁺ T cells directly — our highest-priority next step.\n\n## 9.1 Establishing and validating the TROP2-positive target model\n\n**Aim:** An in vivo experiment is only valid if the tumor cells carry the antigen the vaccine targets. The panel was changed accordingly. In addition, **CD62L is sensitive to temperature and enzymatic dissociation and is readily shed**, so ice-cold buffers were used throughout and prolonged time at room temperature was avoided — otherwise the central memory fraction is underestimated.\n\n**Technical points for Panel ④.** IFN-γ is detectable only after **re-stimulation with PMA (50 ng/mL) + ionomycin (1 µg/mL) for 4–6 h with BD GolgiPlug (1 µL/mL) blocking secretion**; Granzyme B is constitutively stored in cytoplasmic granules and can be stained without re-stimulation. After fixation/permeabilisation, **all washes and antibody dilutions must use Perm/Wash Buffer** — using ordinary staining buffer washes out the saponin, resealing the membrane and producing false-negative intracellular staining.\n\n  \n**Sample size and animal allocation:**\n\n**This section uses n = 3 animals per group — the same 3 as §9.3.** All five metrics have exactly three values per group in the raw data, below the n = 5 of tumor measurement in §9.2. The allocation, as ARRIVE requires it to be stated: **tumor measurement used all 5 animals per group; 3 of those 5 were then selected at random per group, and each of those 3 supplied both the spleen for this section and the tumor section for §9.3.** That is a defensible design (splenocyte preparation plus four staining panels is laborious), but it means the §9.3 images and the measurements in this section come from the same animals rather than from independent cohorts.\n\n**Limitations.** (i) The fundamental species–HLA mismatch still applies — the T-cell responses observed here cannot be shown to be **antigen-specific**; rigorous proof requires tetramer staining. (ii) ICS used **polyclonal** PMA/ionomycin re-stimulation rather than peptide re-stimulation, so it measures **overall** effector capacity, not vaccine-epitope-specific responses. (iii) **n = 3 per group, with limited power**; all five metrics survive Holm correction, but at n = 3 a single animal's variation moves the conclusion materially — Granzyme B already shows this. (iv) **The memory readout is a phenotype, not a function.** CD44⁺CD62L⁺ identifies central-memory-like cells by surface markers; it does not demonstrate recall capacity, which would require re-challenge or an adoptive-transfer experiment. The same caveat applies to the human CD45RO⁺CCR7⁻ data in §6.4.\n\n# Discussion\n\n## Integrated Findings\n\nThis study presents CirCure as a multi-antigen circular RNA vaccine strategy for TNBC, supported by results spanning target selection, construct generation, immune-function assays and an in vivo proof of concept. The dry-lab screen narrowed a broad peptide set to candidates for experimental testing, while the Round 1 assays showed that predicted candidates differed in measured immune activity. These findings informed the selection of epitopes from TROP2, B7-H4, Nectin-4 and LIV-1 for the Combined vaccine.\n\nThe combined vaccine was produced with a high measured circular fraction and increased several immune-response readouts, including dendritic-cell maturation, T-cell activation, effector-memory phenotype and IFN-γ secretion. In vitro, vaccine-primed cells showed strong killing of MDA-MB-231 tumor cells and substantially lower killing of the normal cell lines tested. The in vivo study also showed reduced tumor burden relative to the empty-vector control in the TROP2-overexpressing 4T1 model. \n\nTogether, these findings support the feasibility of combining multiple tumor-associated epitopes in a circular RNA construct and provide preclinical evidence for further evaluation of this approach.\n\n## Potential Relevance to Patients\n\nFor patients with TNBC, a treatment that could address residual tumor cells after surgery would be valuable, particularly where tumor cells differ in the antigens they express. By including epitopes from four tumor-associated antigens, CirCure is designed to reduce reliance on a single target. If this multi-antigen strategy proves effective in further studies, it could offer a way to broaden immune recognition of residual disease and complement surgery and existing treatments.\n\nThe low killing observed in the tested normal cell lines, alongside stronger killing of the TNBC cell line, is encouraging as an initial indication of selectivity within this assay panel. It does not establish safety in patients or show that recurrence can be prevented. Likewise, the animal study supports continued investigation of the approach but did not model treatment after surgical removal of a tumor. CirCure should therefore be viewed as a preclinical therapeutic direction, not as a demonstrated patient benefit.\n\n## Limitations and Future Optimization\n\nThe in vivo model used wild-type BALB/c mice, whereas the vaccine epitopes were designed for human HLA-A\\*02:01*. This mismatch limits conclusions about whether the observed* anti-tumor *response was driven by the intended HLA-restricted epitopes. Testing CirCure in an HLA-A*02:01-transgenic or humanized model, alongside peptide–MHC tetramer analysis, could determine whether the vaccine induces the intended antigen-specific CD8⁺ T-cell response.\n\nThe human immune-cell experiments were limited to in vitro assays, and the study did not include a postoperative residual-disease model. Further validation across independent human donors and in models that better reproduce the human tumor–immune environment is needed.\n\nFurther work could also test the fully formulated circRNA-LNP product in safety studies, since the current LNP dose experiment assessed the empty carrier. Expanding epitope selection beyond HLA-A\\*02:01 would address the limited HLA coverage of the current design and could make the approach relevant to a broader patient population. These studies would help define the conditions under which CirCure’s multi-antigen design may be useful and what changes are needed before its therapeutic potential can be assessed more directly.\n\nOverall, CirCure provides preclinical support for continued development of a multi-antigen circular RNA vaccine strategy for TNBC. Its potential value lies in the possibility of broadening tumor recognition and contributing to postoperative treatment strategies; establishing these benefits will require further testing in models that better reflect human immunity and the post-surgical setting.\n\n# References\n\n1.
