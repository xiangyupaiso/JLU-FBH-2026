# CirCure Human Practices Field Kit

This compact kit turns stakeholder engagement into an auditable project decision. Adapt it to local law, institutional oversight, community expectations, and participant vulnerability. It is not a substitute for ethics or institutional review.

## 1. Stakeholder map

For every candidate stakeholder, record:

| Field | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Relationship | How could CirCure affect them? How could they affect CirCure? |
| Perspective | Lived experience, clinical, technical, ethical, regulatory, implementation, access, or other? |
| Influence | Which project decision can they credibly inform? |
| Vulnerability | Could participation create privacy, health, social, or professional risk? |
| Representation | Whose perspective could this person not represent? |
| Missing voices | Who is still absent, and what does that absence prevent us from claiming? |

Do not use stakeholder prestige as the main selection criterion. Select for relevance, affectedness, disagreement, and perspective diversity.

## 2. Engagement protocol

Before the conversation:

1. State the decision or assumption being tested.
2. Explain why this stakeholder is relevant to that question.
3. Choose an appropriate consent and privacy tier.
4. Separate questions about lived experience from questions requiring technical authority.
5. Prepare at least one question that invites criticism or a no-go condition.

Core interview prompts:

- What part of our framing is wrong or incomplete?
- Who could benefit, be burdened, be excluded, or be harmed?
- Which assumption would have to be true for this approach to be useful?
- What would make you reject or stop this approach?
- What should change in the design, experiment, claim, or implementation plan?
- Who should we speak with next, especially someone likely to disagree?

## 3. Consent and evidence tiers

| Tier | Public page | Internal record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public attribution | Name, role, approved quotation, approved image | Full notes and consent record |
| Role attribution | Role and approved quotation or summary | Identity, full notes, and consent record |
| Anonymized summary | Non-identifying influence summary only | Identity and restricted raw notes |
| Restricted | No public content | Access-controlled research record only |

Consent must cover the actual intended use. Permission to join a conversation is not automatically permission to publish a name, image, health context, or quotation.

## 4. Decision ledger

Use one row per meaningful signal:

| ID | Date | Project layer | Before | Stakeholder signal | Evidence type | Decision | Reason | Project artifact changed | Status | Return step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Example | YYYY-MM-DD | Purpose / Design / Execution / Governance / Implementation | Original assumption | Consent-safe summary | Lived experience / expert opinion / literature / user preference / experimental evidence | Accept / reject / defer | Why this evidence is sufficient or insufficient | Construct, assay, model, language, protocol, implementation | Documented / pending / unresolved | Who reviews the changed version and when? |

Rules:

- Record rejected advice as carefully as accepted advice.
- Do not convert one person's preference into a population-level conclusion.
- Do not convert expert opinion into experimental proof.
- Link every claimed project change to the artifact that actually changed.

## 5. Conflict and decision rule

When stakeholders disagree, document:

1. The competing values or constraints.
2. The evidence base and authority behind each view.
3. Who bears the risk if the team chooses incorrectly.
4. The decision rule used by the team.
5. The minority or unresolved view that remains visible.

A clean consensus story is less credible than a transparent, reasoned decision.

## 6. Return-loop checklist

- Show the stakeholder the changed design, not just a summary of the first meeting.
- Ask whether the change answers the original concern.
- Record agreement, disagreement, and new concerns separately.
- Mark remaining gaps as future questions rather than achieved outcomes.
- Update the decision ledger and the affected technical page.

## 7. Wiki publication gate

Before the wiki freeze, verify that:

- every number has a source and denominator;
- every quotation is verbatim and consent-cleared;
- every stakeholder title is accurate and appropriately specific;
- every evidence code resolves to a real record;
- every claimed project change is visible in a versioned artifact;
- literature, expert opinion, preference, and experimental evidence are visually distinguished;
- limitations, missing voices, negative results, and unresolved conflicts are explicit;
- the page contains no medical efficacy, safety, or readiness claim beyond the available evidence.
