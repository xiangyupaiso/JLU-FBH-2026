A project
taking shape.

The six folios below bring together early openings, paintings and interface studies. Across them, we kept returning to related questions: where the patient belongs in the story, how the circle connects its scenes and how much space an explanation needs. The studies show different ways we approached those questions.

  3. 03

    July 2026Human Practices interaction studies

    Conversations along a timeline

    For Human Practices, the design question was how to connect a conversation with the change it prompts. These three prototypes explore that relationship through a central timeline, a rotating dial and a horizontal card row. The arrangements place different emphasis on chronology, the active conversation and its proposed consequence.

    The prototypes use illustrative portraits and placeholder dates, interview text and figures.

    Centered timeline

    Keeping the sequence visible

    Dates run down the center, with cards alternating to either side. A portrait and role introduce each perspective, followed by a short summary and a longer note. Keeping the dates on one line gives the conversations a clear sequence.

    Try the original interaction · Centered timeline

    Follow the dates down the page. Select “View evidence” to open the demonstration note, then close it to return to the card.

    Open this prototype in a full window ↗

    Rotating timeline

    A fixed place to focus

    The rotating study keeps a date list beside one shared reading position. Scrolling brings each card into that position and updates the highlighted date alongside it. This separates the conversation in focus from its place in the wider sequence, with both visible together.

    Try the original interaction · Rotating timeline

    On a wide screen, scroll inside the demo to turn the wheel, then reverse direction. Open the active card’s note with “View evidence”. On narrow screens, the cards form a linear list.

    Open this prototype in a full window ↗

    Horizontal impact cards

    What each conversation could change

    Each card pairs an image and short statement with a proposed design change. Vertical scrolling moves the row horizontally beneath a five-stage guide. Selecting a phase takes the reader directly to that part of the sequence.

    The final few decisions

    A stable edge for reading

    Revision note

    The first layout places text links in two columns. We then tried lambs and visible descriptions along curved rows. The later revision keeps the illustrations, aligns the titles along straight edges and shows the extra description beside the active choice. This gives the names a steady place to read and the artwork more space.

    A letter with room to breathe

    Revision note

    We gave the desktop letter separate columns for the illustration and message, with more space around each. The team photograph remains visible around the paper, keeping the note connected to its opening scene.
