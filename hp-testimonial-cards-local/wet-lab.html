00

Orientation / claim discipline

What this page proves—and what it does not.

A readable Wet Lab page should make it difficult to confuse a rationale, a planned experiment, a measured result, and a therapeutic claim.

Design established A five-gate experimental sequence

Each claim names a readout, a critical control, and the decision it can support.

Evidence required Traceable records and real figures

Notebook dates, sample identities, replicates, units, statistics, raw files, and limitations.

Not claimed Safety or therapeutic efficacy

The current concept is not evidence of clinical benefit, safety, or deployability.

Why the order matters

A reporter signal cannot establish circular topology. Circular topology cannot establish antigen presentation. Presentation cannot establish selective killing. The page architecture preserves those boundaries.

01

Gate 01 / Construct identity

Prove what was built before asking what it does.

Sequence identity, epitope order, linker boundaries, and the circularization cassette define the test article used by every later experiment.

planned
Claim under test

The assembled plasmid matches the intended, versioned CirCure construct.

Experimental record 01 Identity assay bundle
DDesign

Define expected insert size, sequence boundaries, reading frame, and acceptance criteria before cloning.

BBuild

Assemble candidate clones and preserve a versioned plasmid map linked to a sample ID.

TTest

Use colony PCR, restriction digest, and Sanger sequencing across critical junctions.

LLearn

Advance only sequence-confirmed clones; revise assembly strategy when recurrent errors appear.

Critical control
Verified backbone and expected-size reference
Primary record
Annotated chromatogram + versioned sequence file
Decision rule
No downstream RNA production from an unconfirmed clone
Evidence link
Notebook date, sample ID, Registry entry — awaiting team record
02

Gate 02 / Circularization

A resistant band is not the whole proof.

Circular topology should be supported by an orthogonal evidence bundle. RNase R response is interpreted beside a linear comparison, a back-splice junction readout, and product-purity evidence.

planned
Claim under test

The intended product is circular and distinguishable from linear or concatemeric by-products.

Experimental record 02 Circularization and topology
DDesign

Predefine junction primers, linear comparator, treatment conditions, and a topology acceptance rule.

BBuild

Produce normalized RNA inputs and document purification, recovery, and sample lineage.

TTest

Combine RNase R response with junction-specific detection and sequence confirmation.

LLearn

If the bundle is ambiguous, revise purification or cassette geometry before measuring translation.

Critical control
Matched linear RNA exposed to the same workflow
Primary record
Raw gel/image, junction chromatogram, quantified recovery
Decision rule
Advance only when orthogonal readouts agree
Limit
RNase R resistance alone does not uniquely establish topology
03

Gate 03 / Expression

Measure translation beside delivery and viability.

Expression is a prerequisite—not proof of presentation or therapeutic function. A useful experiment separates successful delivery, translation, and cell stress within the same defined window.

planned
Claim under test

Cells receiving the sequence-confirmed RNA produce the intended encoded material.

Experimental record 03 Expression and viability
DDesign

Define a dose and time range with an acceptable viability window before testing.

BBuild

Prepare normalized circular, matched linear, mock, and positive reporter inputs.

TTest

Read expression and viability from matched samples with documented normalization.

LLearn

Select conditions by signal-to-stress balance, not the maximum reporter value alone.

Critical controls
Mock, linear RNA, empty payload, positive reporter
Possible readouts
Reporter, Western blot, microscopy, or flow cytometry
Decision rule
Advance a reproducible signal inside the predefined viability window
Limit
Expression does not establish antigen processing or MHC-I display
04

Gate 04 / Antigen presentation

Make the immune-readable step visible.

General dendritic-cell activation and sequence-specific MHC-I presentation are different claims. The experiment and the page must report them separately.

future
Claim under test

Antigen-presenting cells process the construct and display the intended epitope through MHC-I.

Experimental record 04 Presentation and APC state
DDesign

Pair a sequence-specific presentation assay with separate APC activation markers.

BBuild

Prepare intended, irrelevant-epitope, no-RNA, and stimulation-control conditions.

TTest

Measure MHC-I presentation and general maturation without collapsing the readouts.

LLearn

Reject conclusions that infer specific presentation from general activation alone.

Critical control
Irrelevant epitope with matched RNA and delivery conditions
Primary record
Gating strategy, raw files, compensation, replicate structure
Decision rule
Specific presentation must exceed the irrelevant-epitope comparison
Dependency
Gate 03 expression evidence must be interpretable first
05

Gate 05 / Functional recognition

The final claim is selective function.

Cytokine release, target-cell killing, and dose-response behaviour belong here. Antigen-negative cells are essential for separating intended recognition from non-specific cytotoxicity.

future
Claim under test

Immune effectors respond preferentially to antigen-positive target cells.

ComparisonQuestion answeredInterpretation boundary
Antigen-positive TNBCDoes the intended response increase?Activity, not yet specificity
Antigen-negative TNBCIs the response antigen-dependent?Separates recognition from general toxicity
No-effector controlWhat is baseline target viability?Defines assay background
Irrelevant-epitope controlIs the response sequence-specific?Tests a competing explanation
Experimental record 05 Functional assay plan
DDesign

Predefine specificity, activity, and viability endpoints with stop/go criteria.

BBuild

Prepare antigen-positive and antigen-negative targets under matched conditions.

TTest

Measure function, target viability, and dose response with suitable baselines.

LLearn

Carry non-specific effects back to construct, dose, and delivery decisions.

Possible readouts
Cytokine release, killing, activation markers, dose response
Critical control
Antigen-negative target under matched assay conditions
Decision rule
Function must be reproducible and distinguishable from non-specific toxicity
Boundary
In-vitro function alone cannot establish clinical safety or efficacy

Protocols, provenance, reuse

Make the method reusable, not merely visible.

Protocols stay folded until needed, keeping the narrative readable while preserving the detail expected from an open-science project record.

PR–01Construct assembly & sequence verificationawaiting team record

Record backbone, insert boundaries, primer sequences, expected sizes, assembly conditions, deviations, sequence files, and clone-to-sample lineage.

Required attachments · annotated plasmid map · chromatograms · notebook date · Registry ID
PR–02RNA production, purification & topologyawaiting team record

Record transcription input, circularization route, cleanup, recovery, integrity, RNase R conditions, primer orientation, back-splice junction sequence, and product-quality limitations.

Required attachments · raw image · quantified recovery · junction trace · sample lineage
